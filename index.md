This site is for researchers and PhD students in economics and finance. It grew out of a talk I gave at a Columbia Business School faculty lunch in October 2026, and it is the companion to my MBA elective, [Finance in the Age of AI](https://ai-finance-cbs.github.io/).

The site is organized around three questions, each with its own tab. (1) How does AI change how I do research? (2) How does AI change what I research? (3) Does AI change whether I do research?

The three questions follow from three features of AI. First is that it's **alien**. It never fatigues, so it can repeat a task thousands of times at the same quality. Its human-like output triggers aversion, and many readers discount work they know a machine produced. It bears no legal liability, so a person still has to stand behind every result. These limits decide which parts of research AI can take over, the subject of [AI as a Substitute]({{ "/substitute/narrative/" | relative_url }}).

Second is that it's **encyclopedic**. It is trained on the entire corpus of human knowledge, errors included. It can answer almost any question a researcher asks, so the scarce skill becomes knowing which questions to ask. [AI as a Topic]({{ "/topic/information-extraction/" | relative_url }}) sorts the new questions that AI opens up into five kinds, each with an example paper.

Third is that it's **generative**. It extracts patterns and reproduces them with high fidelity, good patterns and bad ones alike, so the quality of its output depends on the patterns it is given. [AI as a Tool]({{ "/tool/overview/" | relative_url }}) covers the six ways researchers use it. [Infrastructure]({{ "/infrastructure/data/" | relative_url }}) covers the patterns worth writing down once: data, code, and taste.

The hope is that this site lowers the fixed costs of using AI well for other researchers, and starts a conversation about two open questions: what a referee should assume about how a paper was written, and what PhD training should look like now.
