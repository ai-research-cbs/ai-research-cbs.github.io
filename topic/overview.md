The research questions that AI opens up fall into five kinds. They are ordered by how much of the underlying AI a researcher needs to know, from least to most. Each kind has its own page with an example paper.

<div class="taxonomy">
<p class="tax-scale">Knowledge of underlying AI required</p>
<p class="tax-end">Less</p>
<ol class="tax-list">
<li><span class="tax-num">01</span><h3 class="tax-title"><a href="{{ "/topic/information-extraction/" | relative_url }}">Information Extraction</a></h3><p class="tax-label">Build measures</p><p class="tax-text">Use AI to turn unstructured data into economically meaningful measures.</p><p class="tax-example">Example: Mainardi and Oh (2026)</p></li>
<li><span class="tax-num">02</span><h3 class="tax-title"><a href="{{ "/topic/economic-implications/" | relative_url }}">Economic Implications</a></h3><p class="tax-label">Study AI's effects</p><p class="tax-text">Study how AI adoption changes behavior, markets, and welfare.</p><p class="tax-example">Example: Mainardi, Noh, Oh, and Shin (2026)</p></li>
<li><span class="tax-num">03</span><h3 class="tax-title"><a href="{{ "/topic/modeling-complex-relationships/" | relative_url }}">Modeling Complex Relationships</a></h3><p class="tax-label">Capture complexity</p><p class="tax-text">Use AI to uncover relationships that conventional models miss.</p><p class="tax-example">Example: Kaniel, Lin, Pelger, and Van Nieuwerburgh (2023)</p></li>
<li><span class="tax-num">04</span><h3 class="tax-title"><a href="{{ "/topic/benchmarking-human-decisions/" | relative_url }}">Benchmarking Human Decisions</a></h3><p class="tax-label">Understand people</p><p class="tax-text">Use AI as a benchmark to better understand how humans think and make decisions.</p><p class="tax-example">Example: Bhagwat, Cookson, Dim, and Niessner (2026)</p></li>
<li><span class="tax-num">05</span><h3 class="tax-title"><a href="{{ "/topic/intellectual-transfer/" | relative_url }}">Intellectual Transfer</a></h3><p class="tax-label">Develop theory</p><p class="tax-text">Draw on ideas from AI to develop new economic theories and mechanisms.</p><p class="tax-example">Example: Gabaix, Koijen, Richmond, and Yogo (2025)</p></li>
</ol>
